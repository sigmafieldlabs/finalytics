How we worked it out

Market scenarios
We simulated 1,000 possible paths for the markets, month by month, by replaying blocks of past monthly returns, about 12 months at a time, in a random order (a stationary block bootstrap). The past returns are the assets' monthly returns from Nov 1996 to Oct 2026.
Strong, average and weak markets
The average market is the middle scenario (the median). Only 1 scenario in 10 does better than the strong market (the 90th percentile), and only 1 in 10 does worse than the weak market (the 10th).
Chance
The share of scenarios in which the value at the start of June 2030 is at least $150,000.
Status
Room to spare when the chance is at least 90%; on track when it is at least the 80% you want; nearly on track when it is up to 15 points below it; off track below that. The thin green arc on the ring marks the on-track zone.
Today's money
Returns are after inflation (2.5% a year) and fees (0.25% a year), so every amount is in today's money: $150,000 in 2030 buys what $150,000 buys today. Taxes aren't included.
Getting on track
Each change is worked out on the same scenarios, one at a time: the smallest change that gives an 80% chance.
Spending
Spending is paid as far as the money goes: Car is paid in full in 100% of scenarios.
What if markets do worse
Lower returns: every scenario earns 1 percentage point a year less. A crash: the markets of the worst 12 months in the data for your mix (Dec 2007 to Nov 2008) replace the same months of every scenario.
Worst year and largest fall
Worst year in 20: only 1 twelve-month stretch in 20 of the scenarios loses more. Largest fall: the typical largest fall from a peak over the plan's 4 years, before any money is paid in or out. Volatility: of the mix's monthly returns in the history, a year, before inflation.
What this can't tell you
The scenarios only replay months that happened, so none has a month worse than the worst in the data. The chance is an estimate from past markets, not a promise.
