How we worked it out

Market scenarios
We simulated 200 possible paths for the markets, month by month, by replaying blocks of past monthly returns, about 6 months at a time, in a random order (a stationary block bootstrap). The past returns are the assets' monthly returns from Nov 1996 to Oct 2026.
Strong, average and weak markets
The average market is the middle scenario (the median). Only 1 scenario in 10 does better than the strong market (the 90th percentile), and only 1 in 10 does worse than the weak market (the 10th).
Today's money
Returns are after inflation (2.0% a year), so every amount is in today's money: 10,000 in 2040 buys what 10,000 buys today. Taxes aren't included.
What if markets do worse
Lower returns: every scenario earns 1 percentage point a year less. A crash: the markets of the worst 12 months in the data for your mix (Dec 2007 to Nov 2008) replace the same months of every scenario.
Worst year and largest fall
Worst year in 20: only 1 twelve-month stretch in 20 of the scenarios loses more. Largest fall: the typical largest fall from a peak over the plan's 14 years, before any money is paid in or out. Volatility: of the mix's monthly returns in the history, a year, before inflation.
What this can't tell you
The scenarios only replay months that happened, so none has a month worse than the worst in the data. The chance is an estimate from past markets, not a promise.
